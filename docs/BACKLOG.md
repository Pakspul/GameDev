# Game backlog

The next games, as a list to pick from. Two parts: **ten puzzle games** that fit the collection as it is, and **one bigger, chaotic action game** for when the collection needs something to *play* rather than *solve*.

All names are working titles. "Plays like" names a well-known game in the same genre so we know what we mean; it is a reference, not something to copy. Rules and genres are free; names, art, characters and levels are not (see [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md#game-ideas)).

Ordered roughly by value for effort: high on the list = ad-heavy genre in the stores, endless generated levels, and lots of reuse.

## Part 2: a chaotic action game

### The idea in one line

Overcooked and RV There Yet? are fun because of **many urgent jobs, too few hands, and things going wrong at the worst moment**. With friends, the chaos comes from each other. Alone, it has to come from the **level itself**. So: one character, a thumbstick and two buttons, short generated levels, and a world that keeps throwing problems at you.

### Does this already exist, and is it ruined by ads?

- **Console/PC:** yes, and well done. Overcooked, Moving Out, PlateUp! (which plays fine solo and has generated restaurants), Lovers in a Dangerous Spacetime (run between stations of a ship). None of them are on phones.
- **Mobile, "time management" cooking** (Cooking Fever, Cooking Madness and many clones): you tap stations, you don't walk around. Energy, gems, boosters and ads everywhere.
- **Mobile, "joystick idle"** (My Mini Mart, Burger Please!, Pizza Ready! and dozens of clones): exactly the controls we want (walk with a thumbstick, carry stacks, serve customers), but there is no skill and no chaos. They are idle games, and among the most ad-heavy on the store: an ad after almost every upgrade, "watch to double".

So the controls are familiar to phone players, but **a skill-based, chaotic, short-level, ad-free joystick game is a real gap**. That fits the mission.

### What makes chaos work alone

1. **One character, many stations.** You run between them; the fun is in the route you choose and the shortcuts you find.
2. **The level is your opponent.** A seeded deck of events: a fire, a leak, a conveyor speeding up, a door that jams, a rush order, the floor tilting. The deck gets meaner as the level goes on.
3. **Throwing.** The best verb in Overcooked. Throw the thing to where it needs to be instead of walking it there.
4. **Helpers you build, not teammates you control.** Between levels you choose upgrades (a conveyor belt, a robot arm, an extra counter) that take one boring job off your hands. That gives a roguelite run without anything to buy.
5. **Short levels.** Two to four minutes, one to three stars, a daily seed, an endless mode. A level timer is a game mechanic and fine; anything that stops you playing is not (see [MISSION.md](../MISSION.md)).
6. **Forgiving controls.** Grab snaps to the nearest useful thing, throws auto-aim at the matching station, generous hitboxes. Phones are less precise than a gamepad.

|---|---|---|---|

**Recommendation: Camper Crew.** It takes the two games you like and turns the co-op into "one person running a moving vehicle", which is a proven solo formula (one crew member running between stations). The road gives endless generated levels for free, and the inside of a camper is small, which suits a phone screen. Moving Day is a strong second because of the packing puzzle.

### How we would build it

- **Look and engine:** low-poly 3D from a fixed, slightly tilted top-down camera, so the whole room fits on the screen and you never fight the camera. Per the roadmap, a new game starts on modern three.js rather than r128. Physics: our own simple boxes and gravity first; a small physics library only if we truly need one (it must be offline and phone-home free).
- **Controls:** landscape. Left thumb: floating thumbstick. Right thumb: **Grab/Drop** and **Use** (hold to throw). Also keyboard and a real gamepad (the browser Gamepad API), so a phone with a Bluetooth controller works too.
- **Levels:** a seed builds the road (or house, or kitchen) from hand-made pieces, then draws the event deck. Same seed, same level: daily challenge without a server.
- **Battery:** this is the first game that has to render all the time while playing, so pause hard when paused or in the background, and cap the frame rate.

### First step: is it fun?

Before any art or content, build a **grey-box prototype in a day or two**: one room on a moving floor, thumbstick and two buttons, three stations, three random events, a two-minute timer. Play it on the phone. If running around in grey boxes is already a bit stressful and funny, it is worth building. If not, change the verbs before anything else.
